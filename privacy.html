Luddite HM

Privacy Policy

Last updated October 8, 2026

Luddite HM is a full-screen writing page for classes at Horace Mann School, run by Jake Goldwasser, a teacher there, at jakegoldwasser-hm.github.io/luddite-hm. It is in beta. This page explains what it keeps and why. It's short because Luddite HM keeps very little.

Signing in

You sign in with your Google account, usually your Horace Mann one. Luddite HM receives your name, email address and Google's ID for your account, and uses them only to know who you are: so your teacher can let you into a writing session, and so your papers have your name on them. Luddite HM never sees your password.

What Luddite HM keeps

  • Writing sessions you join: your name and email in that session's list, whether you're waiting, let in or removed, and when you asked to join. If a teacher puts your email address on a class, it is stored with that class so you can be let in on your own. A teacher can also give you your own time (a different start or extra time).
  • While you write: your word count and how many times you left the full-screen page, so your teacher can see that you're writing. A backup of your draft is kept so a crashed or closed browser doesn't lose it.
  • What you hand in: the text, its word count and the time. It's kept so your teacher can read it, mark it up and print it. It's shown to your teacher and to Horace Mann's Luddite HM administrators (below).
  • Your teacher's mark-up on your papers: comments, cross-outs, write-ins, arrows, the end comment and the grade.
  • Teachers' settings: a teacher's display name, classes, and what students get while writing.

Horace Mann administrators

People in Horace Mann's administration whom the school names as Luddite HM administrators can read everything kept above for Horace Mann classes: every teacher's writing sessions, prompts, classes and rubrics, students' backed-up drafts, handed-in papers, and teachers' mark-up, end comments and grades, including writing sessions a teacher has deleted from their list. They can only read: they can't change, mark up or hand back anything. Every page an administrator opens is recorded in an access log (who, what and when) that the administrators can see. Administrators must have an @horacemann.org account. Cassandra Parets is the primary administrator; she and Jake Goldwasser can name others, but Luddite HM gives Jake no way to see students' or teachers' work himself, other than his own classes'. This applies only to Luddite HM, not to Luddite on jake-goldwasser.com.

Teachers and Google Drive

A teacher can connect their Google Drive so that each paper is also saved as a Google Doc in a folder in their Drive. Luddite HM asks only for Google's drive.file permission, which lets it create and see the files it makes itself, and nothing else in the teacher's Drive. It stores a token so it can save those files while the teacher is away; the teacher can remove it at any time from their Google Account's security settings.

What Luddite HM doesn't do

  • No ads and no tracking scripts. Nothing is sold, or shared with anyone other than your own teacher and the Horace Mann administrators described above.
  • Nothing you write is used to train AI models.
  • Its use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Where it's stored

Everything Luddite HM keeps is in one place: a database on Amazon Web Services (AWS), in Amazon's US East (Northern Virginia) region, in an AWS account belonging to Jake Goldwasser. Your browser talks to it only through one small program on AWS (an AWS Lambda function called luddite-api), always over encrypted (HTTPS) connections, and Amazon keeps the database encrypted on its disks. That program and database also serve Luddite, the same writing page on jake-goldwasser.com, so a teacher sees the same writing sessions in both.

Because the database is in his AWS account, Jake could in principle open it directly through Amazon's own tools. He doesn't, and Luddite HM itself shows him nothing beyond his own classes. If the school would rather hold the data itself, it can be moved to an AWS account Horace Mann owns.

Besides AWS, Luddite HM uses:

  • GitHub Pages, which serves the page itself. No writing is stored there.
  • Google, to sign you in, for the page's fonts, and, for teachers who connect it, to save papers to their Drive. Copies saved to a teacher's Drive are in that teacher's Google account.
  • Your own browser, which keeps your sign-in and a copy of what you're writing, so a reload doesn't lose it. Signing out removes the sign-in.

While a student writes in a writing session, the page sends the server their word count every 15 seconds, with a backup of the writing when it has changed, and tells it when they leave the full-screen page. Teachers' pages check the server every few seconds for students' progress and save mark-up as it's made. Nothing else is sent.

Deleting your information

A teacher can delete a writing session, which removes it from their list. To have your information or papers deleted entirely, email jake_goldwasser@horacemann.org and they will be removed within 30 days. Files already saved in a teacher's Drive belong to that teacher.

Students

Luddite HM is meant to be used in class, with a writing session a teacher has set up. Questions about how a class uses it can go to that class's teacher.
